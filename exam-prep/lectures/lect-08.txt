EA: Inheritance
Thus, generating offspring means to create new individuals
with new genomes.
Inheritance in the context of Evolutionary Algorithms
means to use the genomes of those parents that have been
selected.
Inheritance is the principle of transporting some of the
information from the previous generation (parents),
into the next generation,
by generating the offspring out of the parents genomes .
There is a wide variety of possible ways to generate offspring.

EA:
Inheritance can involve k=(0),1, 2, 3, ... or even all m
individuals from the parent generation.
Although, taking k=2 parents is the most popular choice for
implementing inheritance,
the other possibilities are sensible, and are in use.
k=0 parents: no inheritance, generate a novel genome
k=1 parent : just copy the genome of the parent
k=2 parents: recombination,
 e.g. cross-over, mean value, sum, ...
k=3 parents: recombination, e.g. cyclic cross-over
k=m parents: recombination between all parents
Inheritance
© Nils Goerke, University Bonn, 6/2026 31
 Artificial Life [MA-INF 4201], 08, Evolutionary Algorithms 2, Mon 8.6.26, Computer Science University of Bonn
EA:
k=0, no inheritance, generate a novel genome.
The decision to omit inheritance by generating novel genomes
is possible, but not really a good idea for EAs.
Generating new individuals on a random basis, is a very strong
implementation of exploration.
It can be seen as a special variant of Random Search (RS),
(with an extremely large hypersphere).
Typically in EAs, exploration is the role of the subsequent
mutation step.

© Nils Goerke, University Bonn, 6/2026 32
 Artificial Life [MA-INF 4201], 08, Evolutionary Algorithms 2, Mon 8.6.26, Computer Science University of Bonn
EA:
k=1, copy, just copy the genome of the parent.
The decision to make just a copy of one of the parents for
creating the offspring, is not a bad choice for the inheritance
principle.
This is a very strong implementation of exploitation.
Just copying, bears the danger of loosing the (necessary)
diversity among all genomes within the population.
Only the subsequent EA step (mutation) that will potentially
alter the resulting genome, can maintain the diversity to some
extent (exploration).

EA:
Recombination by point wise mean values:
Select two parents, A and B and a random position R within
the genome, and recombine the genomes by building the
mean value between corresponding parts at position R of
the genomes.
Pointwise numeric operations, like:
mean value, sum, difference, maximum or minimum
are only feasible, if the different parts of the genomes are
comparable and numeric.

The job of the mutation step is to maintain the diversity
of the genomes within the population:
Different positions in search space sample the fitness
function at different points.
Changing the genome by mutation is implementing the
exploration principle of stochastic optimization.

Depending on the different way the information is coded in
the genome, the layout of the mutation step is to be
designed differently.
Some common mutation operators are:
Binary genome: a bit-flip
Vector of parameters: change of value
Set of items: replace a single item
String of characters: replace a single character
Sequence: change order within sequence
Other: other method
Special codings of the genome will require that the
mutation is paying respect to this coding.

Mutation
It is possible, that the inheritance and the mutation process
yield invalid genomes, which represent hypotheses s from
outside the allowed search space S.
Those illegal genomes would stress the optimization
process in an unwanted way.
Therefore, it is a good advise, to take precautions against
illegal genomes (if possible, and economic):
- shape the structure of the genome,
- shape the structure of the inheritance,
- shape the structure of the mutation.

EA: External Selection
The purpose of the selection process is to
keep the „Best“, and discard the „Losers“.
The selection is based on the achieved fitness, which is
obtained by the fitness function, quasi from external to the EA.
The m individuals that survive the external selection
process, will be the pool for the parents of the next
generation. Later on, from these m parents the l offspring
will be generated through the inheritance process.
External Selection
© Nils Goerke, University Bonn, 6/2026 83
 Artificial Life [MA-INF 4201], 08, Evolutionary Algorithms 2, Mon 8.6.26, Computer Science University of Bonn
EA:
The two questions for the external selection are:
How many (m) of the individuals shall survive?
and
Which are the ones to keep?
A common way is to keep the population constant to P
individuals.
discard l by external selection
and keep m as pool for the parents,
then generate l offspring for the next generation.

A fitness function that yields a binary value (O,I)
is not a good idea for an evolutionary algorithm.

isolated peeks (I).
The value of this kind of fitness function is not reflecting,
that a genome can be close to a possible solution.

An appropriate fitness function for an EA must be shaped

In addition, the fitness function f value should implement, that
genomes g, close to an optimal solution should yield a larger
fitness value f(g) than those far away.
The optimal would be, to have a fitness function f(g) that is
proportional to the distance between the genome g to an
optimal genome g*.
Unfortunately this is not possible in general.

EA:
The objective, is to find a placement of the 8 queens
so that they can't reach each other.
Example: 8 Queens
A proposal for a fitness function for the 8 queens problem:
Each possibilty that one queen can attack another queen is
counted as -1
The fitness value f(g) is the sum of all attack possibilities.
This yields a graded response as required,
with a maximal value f(g*) = 0.0 when no attack is possible.
Caution:
This fitness function is only appropriate if the number of queens
is fixed to 8 (0 queens => 0 attack possibilities)
