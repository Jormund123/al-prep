\section*{Assignment 56 (2 Points)}
\begin{problem}
    Explain the term \textit{Hamming-Cliffs} with respect to Evolutionary Algorithms. \\
    Discuss the negative and positive aspects of this phenomenon in the context of EAs.\\
    \\
    You are explicitly encouraged to seek the literature about Evolutionary Algorithms to complete the task, provided you give correct reference (citation).
\end{problem}

\begin{answer} % Sebastian
    The Hamming Distance is a distance measure. In EA it can determine the distance between genomes. The distance is measured by counting the amount of differing entries between genomes. A Hamming Cliff refers to an abrupt difference between similar genomes. Genomes are similar when they are close in the problem space. For example, a bit representation of integer numbers includes the similar numbers $3_{10}=011_2, 4_{10}=100_2$, but their hamming distance is maximized, as all entries differ.
\end{answer}

\section*{Assignment 58 (2 Points)}
\begin{problem}
    Name, and describe at least three aspects, why evolutionary algorithms are so popular.
\end{problem}
\begin{answer}
    \begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Broad applicability / no gradient needed.} EAs treat the objective as a black box. They only require a fitness value, not derivatives, continuity, or an analytic form, so they work on discrete, mixed, noisy, non-differentiable, or otherwise ill-behaved problems where classical optimizers fail.

    \item \textbf{Global search robust to local optima.} Working with a population and using stochastic operators (mutation, recombination) lets EAs explore many regions of the search space simultaneously, making them far less likely than local methods to get permanently stuck in a single local optimum.

    \item \textbf{Simplicity, generality, and flexibility.} The basic algorithm is conceptually simple and easy to implement, and the same framework adapts to almost any problem by just redefining the genome encoding and fitness function. They are also inherently parallelizable (fitness evaluations are independent) and can yield multiple good solutions at once.
\end{enumerate}
\end{answer}

\section*{Assignment 59 (2 Points)}
\begin{problem}
    Describe two operators for an evolutionary algorithm: one that is implementing a pure exploration strategy, and a second one implementing a pure exploitation strategy.
\end{problem}
\begin{answer}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Pure exploration operator: macro / random mutation}: For example, a uniform random reset of genes or a large-step mutation. It generates offspring largely independent of the parents' current values, sampling new, distant regions of the search space and introducing fresh genetic material. This widens the search but ignores accumulated quality information.
        \item \textbf{Pure exploitation operator: deterministic rank-based elitist $(\mu + \lambda)$ selection}: It always keeps the best-ranked individuals and discards the worst, with no randomness. This concentrates the search around the currently best solutions, refining them, but contributes no new diversity on its own.
        \end{itemize}
\end{answer}
